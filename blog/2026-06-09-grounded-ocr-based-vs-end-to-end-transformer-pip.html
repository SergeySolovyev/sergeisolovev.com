OCR-Based vs. End-to-End Transformer Pipelines for Receipt Information Extraction (SROIE 2019)

2026-06-09 · Sergei Solovev, HSE University

A common assumption in document AI is that end-to-end models are simply better than cascaded OCR pipelines. The data from this comparison suggest the picture is more nuanced.

Evaluating EasyOCR-based extraction against fine-tuned Donut on the ICDAR 2019 SROIE benchmark — 80 validation receipts, three target fields (vendor, date, total) — neither paradigm dominates. Donut achieves higher overall micro-F1 (0.75 vs. 0.63), but the OCR pipeline outperforms it specifically on date extraction (F1 = 0.78 vs. 0.63), where regex patterns over a closed vocabulary are simply more reliable than learned generation.

The more actionable finding: a 12-category error taxonomy shows the two systems fail on largely non-overlapping document subsets (Pearson r = 0.30). That low failure correlation is a direct argument for ensemble strategies rather than treating the paradigm choice as binary.

On corrupted images, Donut degrades less sharply (ΔF1 = −0.12 vs. −0.17), consistent with the theoretical expectation that errors compound across stages in multi-step systems.

For anyone building document extraction pipelines — invoices, financial records, RAG ingestion of unstructured documents — the practical takeaway is that field type and corruption profile matter more than paradigm when making the architecture decision. Code, data, and trained checkpoints are publicly available.
